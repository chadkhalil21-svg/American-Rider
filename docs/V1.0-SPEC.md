## The core shift: "Friendly" → "Institutional"

The whole direction: it should **not** feel like Uber, a startup, or "friendly/cute."
It should feel like an airline / bank / Amex Centurion / Bloomberg terminal / NASA —
**quiet, confident, institutional, engineered, timeless.** Every screen says: *"This is
America's transportation infrastructure,"* not *"another ride-share app."*

Design rules:
- **Whitespace** is the premium signal. If a screen feels too empty, remove another thing.
- **Cards** almost disappear — subtle separation, no heavy borders/shadows. Architectural.
- **Icons** — almost none. No emoji, no cartoons. Only minimal outlined geometric (SF Symbols style).
- **Color** — white, black, deep navy, one blue, one green, one red. Nothing else.
- **Animation** — slow, elegant, glide/fade. Nothing bounces or spins.
- **Typography is the brand** — SF Pro Display; short, professional sentences; no adjectives, no excitement.
- Design system name: **ARTS™ (American Rider Transportation Standard)**. Values: Clarity, Authority, Minimalism, Consistency, Trust, Precision.
- Palette from his design-system sheet: Deep Navy `#14171F`, Rider Blue `#2E5FE0`, Stone `#8F8F87` (grey).

### Travel Preferences — the reorganization (his best UI idea)
- **Climate:** Cool · Moderate · Warm  (no numbers)
- **Atmosphere:** Quiet · Ambient · Conversation  (drop ambiguous "Personal")
- **Music:** None · Ambient · Traveler Choice → Choose Source (Apple Music / Spotify / YouTube Music / **Operator Playlist**). Operator Playlist = curated ambient list, one-tap opt-in, avoids copyright issues.

### New signature idea — **Travel Modes** (how the platform optimizes the journey)
Direct (fastest, single operator) · Economy (lowest cost, may use rail/transit) ·
Balanced · Quiet · Premium (top operators/vehicles). Ties directly into the
rail/multi-modal expansion vision — traveler picks the goal, AI picks single-operator
vs. multi-modal itinerary.

### Reframe the money pages (numbers-first, unemotional)
- 99% page → **"Operator Economics — 99% (American Rider) vs ~50% (average platform); platform take 1%."**
- Insurance page → **"Revenue Comparison / Commercial Insurance Recovery Estimate":** Monthly Revenue (traditional vs AR) · Difference · Insurance Cost · Net Monthly Gain. Remove "your insurance pays for itself" emotion — let numbers speak. (We already have the calculator; just relabel + de-emotion.)

## Three-app architecture (his one architectural recommendation)
1. **Traveler** — reserve/manage/review travel, simple + elegant.
2. **Operator** — operations, revenue, navigation, qualification, professional tools.
3. **Operations** — internal monitoring, AI analytics, compliance, support.
Same ARTS design language across all three. Lets AR scale Miami → national without
re-architecting.

## Recommended tech stack (his) — vs. what we have
- His minimum launch stack: **Flutter + Firebase + Stripe Connect + Google Maps + Operations Portal.**
- Firebase: Auth, Firestore, Cloud Functions, Cloud Messaging (push), Storage.
- Payments: **Stripe Connect** (marketplace: collect from Traveler, pay Operator as connected account). Cards/Apple/Google first, add ACH/Pay-by-Bank later to cut fees.
- Maps: **Google Maps Platform** (Maps, Places autocomplete, Routes, Route Matrix, Navigation SDK) — usage-priced, minimize calls.
- Real-time matching: Reservation → nearest qualified Operator → request → countdown → Commissioned → Travel.
- Operator onboarding: identity + document upload + admin approval in the portal; automate background-check pass/fail + AI insurance-doc review (policy type / dates / limits), human review only for ambiguous.
- AI features (demand, revenue roadmap, support, safety, micro-surge) come **later — build the working marketplace first.**
- ⚠️ **Note:** he suggests Flutter; our real app is already **React Native / Expo**, which does the identical job. No need to switch and redo work — continue in Expo. (Firebase/Stripe/Google Maps all work with RN/Expo.)

## Key business facts (full detail in the partner's planning summary)
- Fee model: 1% of the travel fare (no cap) + the traveler's platform fee, the greater of $1.50 and 5% of the travel fare (Chad, 9 Sept 2026); operator keeps 99%. Margin per fare on US and international cards: docs/ECONOMICS-AND-INFRASTRUCTURE.md §2.
- Insurance: AR provides **no** platform-side coverage, so operators need a **full standalone commercial "for-hire livery" policy** (~$400–650/mo est., **get real Progressive Commercial quotes**), not the cheap rideshare endorsement. Progressive cheapest; State Farm/Allstate don't do true livery. Compare against a driver's *existing* combined spend, not $0.
- Background checks: **legally required in FL** (HB 221), ~$30–50/driver; driver-pays-vendor flow; platform still the FCRA "user" (consent + adverse-action needed).
- Company/payments: DBA to start, spin up an **LLC** once real liability begins; **Stripe Connect** individual/SSN acceptable to start, migrate later; no setup/monthly fee.
- Legal: FL §627.748 preempts local rules (Miami-Dade can't add its own). Re-verify every state before expansion — nothing transfers automatically.
- Dispute AI "constitution": Tier 1 rules-based (fare/route/duplicate/cancel), Tier 2 AI+evidence (late pickup, cleaning w/ photo), Tier 3 human (safety/injury/police/bank). Written policy, not freeform.
- Safety: 911 first-tier, never AI-gated; 311 non-emergency; 211 social services. AI surfaces the resource, never triages severity.
- Hardware: none — pure software, operators use own phones, cloud scales.
- Two-person team: the real constraint is capacity, not demand — automate verification so onboarding doesn't require a human per driver.

## NEW DIRECTIONS (2026-07-09 — from Adrian + partner)

### Smart Travel (multi-modal) — the missing Travel Option
A signature option to add to **Travel Options**: instead of one point-to-point car, the AI
splits a long congested trip into **Car → Rail/Bus → Car**:
`[Rider] –(AR Leg 1: first mile)→ [Transit Hub] –(Metrorail / Brightline / Tri-Rail / South Dade BRT)→ [Destination Hub] –(AR Leg 2: last mile)→ [Destination]`.
- **Why it wins:** bypasses I-95 / Palmetto gridlock → ~half the time; ~half the cost for the rider; aligns with Miami-Dade's 2026 SMART Plan (partner, not adversary, to transit).
- **Hidden economics:** the platform collects **one** platform fee per journey (on the combined car fare — decided 9 Sept 2026) + 1% on **two** short legs instead of one long ride. Operators get short high-margin "feeder loop" trips near hubs (flip faster, less deadheading), while the rider still pays less.
- **Illustrative unit economics** (Cutler Bay → Downtown, rush hour): Standard pure-car ≈ 65–80 min, $45. Smart Travel ≈ 40–45 min, ~$21.50 ($8 leg 1 + $2.25–5 transit + $8.50 leg 2). Rider saves ~50% + half the time.
- **UI plan:** add a "Smart Travel" row to Travel Options, badged (e.g. "Fastest · Lowest cost"); selecting it shows the 3-leg itinerary (each leg with mode, time, cost) and a combined total. Needs a transit API (Google Routes / GTFS) for real; demo can show a scripted Miami itinerary. **Adrian is sending more details before build — do not build the itinerary screen until they land, to avoid rework.**

### Atmosphere & Music model (resolve the "Ambient" confusion)
Problem Adrian flagged: **"Ambient" appears in BOTH Atmosphere and Music** → ambiguous.
- **Atmosphere** (the human vibe): rename the middle option to remove the clash. Recommended **Quiet · Relaxed · Conversation** (Relaxed = easygoing, light background, not silent but not chatty). Add a one-line description under the control that updates with the selection.
- **Music** (kept separate): **None · Ambient · Traveler Choice.**
  - *Ambient* = American Rider's own **royalty-free calm instrumental playlist** (no lyrics, low volume) — always available, no licensing issues, predictable. (NOT "the operator's own music" — that's operator discretion, kept separate.)
  - *Traveler Choice* = the traveler picks the **source**: connect their own **Apple Music / Spotify / YouTube Music** account, OR pick the **Operator Playlist** (a curated ambient list the operator maintains, one-tap opt-in).
  - Answers to Adrian's questions: a traveler's playlist plays in **that** ride (operator has final say), not broadcast to all operators. YouTube = via YouTube Music as a connected source. Copyright is handled by connecting the user's **own** account (AR never hosts copyrighted playlists); AR only hosts the royalty-free Ambient list.
- Add short one-line descriptions under Atmosphere and Music that update with the selected option, so no label is ambiguous. **Pending Adrian's sign-off on wording before editing the app.**
