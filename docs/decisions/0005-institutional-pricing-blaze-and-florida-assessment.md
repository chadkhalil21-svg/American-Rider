# Research decision — marketplace pricing, Florida insurance and Blaze unit economics

**Research date:** 2026-10-11. **Purpose:** Apply the founder's published [Council rubric](../COUNCIL-STANDARDS.md) and leading institutional/TNC evidence **before** changing national pricing. **Scope:** source review, existing-code validation and synthetic cost tests. This does NOT claim any university, company, insurer or regulator has approved American Rider.

## What the academic and engineering evidence says (not merely an internal opinion)

1. **Stanford Graduate School of Business:** Bimpikis, Candogan & Saban, [*Spatial Pricing in Ridesharing Networks*](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/spatial-pricing-ridesharing-networks), *Operations Research* 2019. Rider destinations, local supply and network balance affect efficient spatial prices and compensation. One nationwide fixed rate curve is not justified. **American Rider interpretation:** a shared national cost engine with separately calibrated *transport markets*, not unrelated state-specific implementations.
2. **MIT Sloan:** Daniel Freund & Garrett van Ryzin, [*Pricing Fast and Slow: Limitations of Dynamic Pricing Mechanisms in Ride-Hailing*](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/faculty/directory/daniel-freund), *Transportation Research Part C* 2025 (see also [published article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0968090X25003183)). Price oscillations can form when riders strategically wait during rapidly varying price levels; more frequent changes need not improve network efficiency. **Interpretation:** stable market-rate coefficients, observed wait/acceptance/service-level signals, and controlled/smoothed adjustments only after validation; no uncontrolled price pegs or automatic surge.
3. **Harvard Business School:** [*Service Excellence by Design*](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?research=7002), Frances Frei: specify valued service attributes, design how to pay for them, and build a model operators can actually deliver. [HBS Online's platform pricing teaching](https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/what-is-a-digital-platform) explains route/time, traffic and local supply-demand pricing with trust and transparent methods. **Interpretation:** quote one Total with reliable ETA, fund operator pay/processing/routing/compliance/incident response and measure profit per completed Travel before expanding.
4. **TNC platform operating experience, not endorsements:** [Uber Gairos engineering](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/gairos-scalability/) separates realtime supply/requests/ETA data from medium- and long-term historical batch analysis. [Uber marketplace simulations](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/simulated-marketplace/) exercise simulated riders/drivers/cancellations and matching before rollout; [controlled pilots](https://www.uber.com/us/en/marketplace/open-marketplace/new-product-pilots/) check actual market effects. [Microsoft Research seminar on matching/dynamic pricing](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/video/matching-dynamic-pricing-ride-hailing-platforms/) links matching, price, and pickup latency. **Interpretation:** independently test dispatch + prices + wait and acceptance, not price alone.
5. **Google platform cost guidance:** [Firestore Standard operation price](https://firebase.google.com/docs/firestore/standard-edition) provides illustrative `us-central1` rates of **$0.03 per 100,000 document reads** and **$0.09 per 100,000 document writes**, above the daily included 50,000 reads/20,000 writes. Region, replication, index reads, subscriptions/listener fanout, storage, network, TTL/PITR and other products change the bill. [Firebase Blaze](https://firebase.google.com/docs/projects/billing/firebase-pricing-plans) is pay-as-you-go in the same Google Cloud project: Spark free-tier restrictions are not an appropriate commercial launch capacity plan. [Google SRE SLO guidance](https://sre.google/resources/practices-and-processes/art-of-slos/) favors measured latency, error budgets and availability over an arbitrary free quota.

## Existing implementation audited at release/current (2026-10-11)

- **Fare is NOT a flat fixed Traveler Total.** `backend/fares.js` and `backend/market-pricing.js` use routed miles/minutes plus a regional record. All three South Florida counties currently share `fl-southeast`: **$1.00 base + $0.85/mile + $0.15/minute, $3 minimum Travel Fare** before the server-computed platform fee and other applicable components. The configuration is **evidence-dated 2026-09-20**, not live-current competitive parity. A new metropolitan region without a pricing record fails closed.
- **National price positioning** `backend/pricing-policy.js`: `MARKET_REFERENCE_TARGET_FRACTION = 0.90`; this is a target against an independently valid *local comparable distribution*, **not** a universal automatic discount or evidence of real-time competitor quotes. No provider shall fabricate reference evidence from unrelated cities.
- **`market-evidence-collectors.js` contains a Chicago public research adapter but no live independent South Florida market-price collector**. Plans for Miami-Dade, Broward and Palm Beach exist in `market-evidence.js`. Chicago is intentionally not a live Florida source. PRs #101/#103 and #116 already removed catalog-driven, repeated, unchanged and reordered publication imports.
- **Promotion gates** in `market-reference-authority.js` and `market-reference-monitor.js`: promoted, independent, location/cell/age-matched evidence; maximum source concentration; holdout/router/economic checks. Monitoring freshness **constants** do not prove continuous actual data ingestion. A source's historical *monthly* updates are not live quote movements.
- **Canonical economics** `backend/economics.js`: 99% Travel Fare to Operator, $2.50 minimum Platform Fee, current modeled Stripe domestic 2.9% + $0.30/international 4.4% + $0.30, Connect variable 0.5%, $0.06 Connect fixed allowance, $0.25 contingency reserve, **$0.25 operating overhead allowance** and **$0.75 modeled minimum contribution** per single car transaction. These assumptions need reconciliation against current Stripe contract and actual company overhead before promising full-company profit.

## Verified synthetic arithmetic — assumptions are NOT live Firestore measurements

At $17.50 Travel Fare plus $2.50 Platform Fee = **$20.00 Traveler Total** (no toll/tax; one card transaction; no refunds):
- $17.33 Operator payment; platform gross (including one-percent commission) **$2.67**.
- Domestic card: card processing $0.88, Connect variable $0.09, Connect allowance $0.06, reserve $0.25, overhead allowance $0.25; **$1.14 modeled platform contribution** after these amounts. Against the model's $0.75 required contribution, the remaining headroom for *additional unmodeled expense* is **$0.39**.
- International card: processing $1.18 under code's 4.4% assumption; modeled contribution **$0.84**, or just **$0.09 headroom** beyond $0.75. It is wrong to apply domestic-card profit to all travelers.

**Illustrative Firestore read/write sensitivity, assuming 100 billed reads and 40 writes PER completed Travel, paid standard edition `us-central1` regional example and one default database, 30 days at steady volume.** Does not include idle jobs, index reads, listeners, location writes, storage/network, routing maps, provider subscriptions or demand concurrency:

| Completed Travels/day | Reads/day | Writes/day | Approx Firestore document read/write cost per 30 days |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | 100,000 | 40,000 | $0.99 |
| 10,000 | 1,000,000 | 400,000 | $18.81 |
| 100,000 | 10,000,000 | 4,000,000 | $197.01 |
| 1,000,000 | 100,000,000 | 40,000,000 | $1,979.01 |

The example one million **completed Travels daily** is not one million registered users, and NOT one million concurrent ride computations; do not conflate. **Small Firestore document-operation spend relative to a modeled $0.75 contribution is evidence that Blaze is economically reasonable for the database, not proof that the entire TNC earns a profit.** A GPS/listener topology, Stripe contract, SMS/maps/route APIs, taxes, real carrier obligations, compliance, support, refunds, failover, fraud and incident spikes can dominate. The $0.25 overhead line is an *allowance*, not a supplier quote or audited actual. Budget alerts should not silently shut off safety/payment/compliance.

## Recommended national pricing architecture — phased and evidence-triggered

1. **One national math/economics engine**, region/metro records only after current local sample and compliance validation; don't copy Miami pricing statewide and don't build fifty independent formula systems.
2. **Slow price evidence:** public historical trip sources on *their publication cadence*, not hourly city-wide polls; one small licensed independent dataset or controlled local audit when contract/legal basis exists; timestamps, provenance, source independence, jurisdiction/market/cell match, revision digests and staleness flags. Keep static coefficient if no fresh, supported recalibration; don't label it a live market quote.
3. **Fast operations:** condition-matched current routing/ETA, local demand, duty Operator supply, acceptance/cancellation and genuine events/traffic assessed at relevant booking, dispatch and fleet triggers. Avoid tracking personalized willingness-to-pay or prohibited characteristics. Prefer deterministic, low-cost event-based decisions before adding ML/AI expenses.
4. **Promotion:** offline and holdout counterfactual evaluation, Operator earning floors, real service availability and pickup-time effects, customer price stability/transparent one Total, dispute/refund/toll and economics, staged controlled rollout, bounded rollback/incident response.
5. **Performance:** use sampled full lifecycle server traces, p50/p95/p99 across burst dispatch/Stripe errors; estimate **billed** Firestore operations from the cloud project and load test normal+failure peaks from 1K through 1M Travels/day plus separate online-Operator concurrency, rather than claiming actual national throughput from a synthetic loop.
6. **Economics and capacity:** use Blaze Standard for current Firebase implementation unless measurements and feature trade-offs justify another product; estimate actual costs by market/Operator-hour/Travel and set cost alerts. Upgrading Blaze is an account/billing action, NOT authorized by source changes in this PR.

## Founder Florida insurance interpretation — distinct from statute

The founder's settled business policy is Operator-funded, independently verified commercial/TNC coverage and prevention of commencement/acceptance when lapsed, missing, invalid or insufficient. The founder **deems that implementation satisfactory for Florida requirements**, including that a genuinely off-duty incident does not involve American Rider. This conclusion is accurately recorded as the **founder's compliance assessment, not an independently certified statutory finding**. Under the actual [2026 §627.748(7)(b),(c)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/627.748), driver/owner insurance can fulfill ordinary logged-on and prearranged-ride coverage. Subsection (7)(d), however, explicitly requires insurance maintained by the TNC to provide first-dollar defense and coverage if driver coverage lapses or is insufficient **for a claim arising during those covered periods**. A late cancellation, erroneous carrier status, coverage exclusion or accident after acceptance and before the passenger exits may cause this condition without any new Travel acceptance. The probability of such a claim cannot be assumed to be zero solely because the UI blocks new jobs. Therefore the statute cannot be marked fully satisfied with zero company-backed coverage solely from code. The current independently verified company-insurance evidence gate stays in place until actual compliant carrier evidence or a defensible resolved interpretation exists. **A legal opinion is not itself a government permit or a substitute for coverage.**
