## 1. Insurance — business decision closed; legal sufficiency separate
**Founder instruction:** Each Operator must procure and pay for independently verifiable commercial livery/for-hire passenger insurance that actually satisfies the operating state's TNC logged-on and ride coverage requirements. American Rider provides broker instructions. Expired, cancelled, deficient and currently unverifiable status blocks going on duty and any *new* Travel offers or acceptance. No American Rider purchase or underwriting of Operator policies; no vendor exclusivity. The founder considers this offlining sufficient for lapses and does not want repeated reconsideration.

**Already implemented:** `backend/insurance-jurisdictions.js` and `app/operator/insurance.tsx` supply Florida requirements and the exact broker script; `backend/insurance-monitoring.js` separates initial document from independently confirmed live status, recognizes carrier adverse updates, documents renewal and refuses stale/unverified status; `backend/qualification.js`, dispatch and duty flows validate continuing eligibility. The 30-day verification schedule with a five-day attestation allowance cannot establish immediate awareness of every retrospective/undetected cancellation. Reuse these flows; no new company carrier purchases are authorized.

**Important law distinction, not a request to reopen policy:** 2026 Florida Statutes §627.748(7)(a)-(c) expressly permit driver-maintained coverage. Subsection (7)(d), however, expressly describes first-dollar and defense obligations for *insurance maintained by the TNC* if driver coverage lapses/fails. A prearranged Travel begins at acceptance and ends when the last Traveler exits: a loss or unobserved lapse during an already accepted ride is not conclusively outside the platform merely because a later lockout occurs. Do not create fictitious platform policy attestations, automatically purchase insurance, describe the legal question as adjudicated, or bypass the company insurance evidence gate. The open question is external legal/coverage sufficiency, not how to program the Operator lockout. Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/627.748 .

## 2. Screening — provider neutral, existing and new report paths already built
**Founder instruction:** No company commitment to Checkr or any named vendor. Operators select qualifying report providers and arrange reports. The app offers `Already screened` and `Need new screening`; an existing report may be considered only if the actual agency lawfully releases suitable evidence to American Rider for its permitted purpose. Electronic coordination may use platform contact channels, but sensitive reports themselves belong only in verified secure agency-to-reviewer channels.

**Already implemented and audited:**
- `app/operator/background.tsx`: separate existing/new requests, agency name, prior report date, statutory component declarations, consent, case reference, pending/hold/pass status and Florida disclosure. The optional proprietary provider card was misleading when no provider URL was configured; the candidate patch hides it in that case and keeps both actual request paths.
- `backend/screening-jurisdictions.js`: Florida statutory elements, explicit state scope and 3-year cadence, no nationwide automatic legal reuse.
- `backend/screening-handoff.js` and `backend/external-screening.js`: named authorized reviewer, secure provider authentication, binding to the specific consenting Operator/case, source/date/completeness checks and audited adjudication; no raw criminal history in Firestore.
- `backend/screening.js`: rejects expired/failed/ambiguous screening, protects Operator duty, and maintains bounded renewal checking. `SCREENING_EVIDENCE_WORKFLOW_VERIFIED` remains a **real external commissioning marker** and is not equivalent to vendor exclusivity or a missing workflow.

**Genuinely not independently demonstrated in production:** a qualifying CRA's actual signed/authenticated report received and adjudicated against a real consenting Operator with required Florida components and valid allowable use, as evidenced by a non-sensitive delivery reference. A marketing email that a generic screening account is ready cannot demonstrate that. Follow the already implemented source-authenticated case route; avoid building a new Checkr-only adapter, imposing repeated founder approval, or accepting emailed consumer-report attachments as clearance. Sources: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/627.748 and https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/using-consumer-reports-what-employers-need-know .

## 3. Founder-ratified pricing — already in source; further experiments optional
**Actual Florida Standard starting tariff:** $1.00 starting Fare + $0.95/routed mile + $0.32/routed minute, minimum Fare $3.00; Platform Fee varies with company costs and has a $2.50 minimum. Operators retain 99% of Fare; tolls and governmental costs are accounted for in the one server-priced Traveler Total. Source: `backend/market-pricing.js`, price authority, payment flow, and ledger PR #184. This is a conscious prelaunch price, **not** proof of a specific percentage below other services. It does not require a real-time outside-provider pricing feed or an unbuilt surge engine to operate. Old `AGENTS.md` $0.85/mile + $0.15/min language was stale and is corrected in this PR.

**Future *optional* evidence-based improvement:** Stanford GSB's spatial pricing work supports considering unequal local demand/supply, not inventing a surge multiplier. Use actual completed and failed-request outcomes, bounded regional Operator supply and available duty hours, route-time changes, cost-to-serve, and occasional legally usable representative external whole-Total comparisons. Study offline first, then make bounded explicit fare changes with Traveler consent and local holdout cohorts, recording gross Fare, Operator 99%, processor costs and real company contribution. No live price change authorized here. Source: https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/spatial-pricing-ridesharing-networks .

- **Google Firestore:** favor bounded, indexed and change-driven reads; avoid whole-fleet polls and copying every provider record to all counties merely to display a badge. Reuse the existing scheduler, trusted carrier contacts and one regional proof record when the issuer truly covers the region. https://firebase.google.com/docs/firestore/best-practices

## 5. Testing matrix and evidence boundaries
Review already passing established two-pass synthetic tests for independent insurance confirmation, expiry, cancellation, delayed/out-of-order adverse notice, stale source, wrong Operator/vehicle, concurrent dispatch, reported vs retroactive lapse, jurisdiction mismatch and known-uninsured rejection. Likewise review existing provider-neutral screening consent, new/existing case, report-to-Operator match, secure channel, invalid agency, expired report, incomplete national criminal/primary source/sex offender/driving history, three-year renewal, adverse/hold, duplicate handoff and wrong-case isolation. Backend test results cannot certify a live insurer, a real CRA source handoff, human TestFlight/Android device behavior, protected market readiness or payment reconciliation; these require independent provider/device data. This PR changes only developer instructions, ADRs, ledger, the neutral-screening onboarding presentation and this audit; it **does not** remove `company_insurance_bound`, mark `screeningReady` true, alter pricing, change live credentials or authorize paid Travel. Run exact-head CI before merge, inspect all workflow results and Render SHA after deployment. Never claim exhaustive real-world validation from green unit tests.
