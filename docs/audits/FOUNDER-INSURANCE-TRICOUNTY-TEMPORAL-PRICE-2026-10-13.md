## Founder's settled BUSINESS rule (retain without repeatedly re-debating design)

1. Every Operator, at their own expense, obtains and maintains independently confirmed commercial livery/for-hire passenger coverage, or equivalent standalone TNC-qualifying coverage, suitable for the jurisdiction, insured Operator/vehicle and all active logged-on and prearranged-ride periods. A mere screenshot/declarations page without independent confirmation is not authoritative proof. Policy instructions appear in `backend/insurance-jurisdictions.js` and the onboarding surfaces consume that jurisdiction configuration. There is no universal policy brand or insurer requirement.
2. For Florida the 2026 Fla. Stat. §627.748(7)(b) logged-on minimum is $50k/$100k/$25k with required PIP/UM treatment; §627.748(7)(c) requires $1m primary liability during a prearranged ride with applicable PIP/UM. Qualification verifies driver/vehicle, use, period-specific limits and effective/expiry dates. Other states remain unconfigured and fail closed. Do not erroneously treat a personal auto insurance policy as TNC coverage.
3. When status is **known** cancelled, expired, nonrenewed, reduced, vehicle removed, unverified or past the independently confirmed status maximum, fail closed at onboarding, starting work, offering and acceptance. The backend already contains the ongoing `continuingStatus`, `assessOperator`, eligibility and online renewal state machines. New tests prove cancellations reported AFTER offer but BEFORE acceptance refuse the Travel and take the Operator offline. Previously accepted/paying or in-progress Travel requires separate lawful incident/claims handling—disabling future requests is not proof of immunity for a prior ride.
4. Founder's legal argument: If the Operator is blocked instantly on known lapse, unrelated driving thereafter should not be treated automatically as American Rider operations. This is the FOUNDER'S POSITION, not a finding of law or a commitment from an insurer. It is the default operating design, not an issue to be repeatedly raised as though no lapse control existed.

**Important statutory distinction:** [2026 Fla. Stat. §627.748(7)(d)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/627.748) explicitly states that **if** the Operator's required coverage lapses OR does not provide coverage, insurance maintained by the TNC must respond first-dollar and defend. §§(7)(b)-(c) allow a driver/owner, company, or combination to satisfy primary insurance, but (7)(d) does not state that automatic offlining relieves the TNC of the duty for any loss during a relevant period. Software cannot guarantee the exact moment a carrier cancels, whether a purported coverage is excluded, whether a collision preceded our notification, or whether insurer coverage is judged insufficient. **Do not label this statutory question legally closed**, fabricate an existing company insurance policy, zero out all contingent costs, admit new markets or authorize live paying rides based on the founder's interpretation alone. Preserve independent `company_insurance_bound` evidence in market commercial activation until Florida-qualified insurance counsel/carrier documents the arrangement. Once evidence is obtained, do not repeat the issue as a generic argument; reference this decision.

## Time of day and calendar — original academic results versus current implementation

- [Columbia, Carnegie Mellon, Harvard: Spatio-Temporal Pricing for Ridesharing Platforms (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.2021.2178) studies **smooth changes across both time and location**, balancing Operator acceptance versus demand. [Stanford GSB: Spatial Pricing in Ridesharing Networks](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/spatial-pricing-ridesharing-networks) shows Operator availability/destination imbalance matters. Columbia has [direct cab price/wait research](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/value-time-evidence-auctioned-cab-rides) estimating different travel-time valuations by work/nonwork hours.
- American Rider currently prices measured miles plus measured **routed minutes**. Peak traffic can therefore increase a route's price if the estimated journey duration increases. It does NOT yet apply an automatic day-of-week/date/surge multiplier just because it's a Friday, holiday or evening. Existing `backend/pricing-policy.js` provides `daypart`, `weekdayWeekend` and `calendarClass` as reference dimensions; their existence is NOT a ready live demand model. The proposed Florida rate has the same feature boundary.
- Recommended simplest improvement AFTER launch data: collect anonymized hourly counts of actual quotes, Operator availability, accepted/completed Travels, waiting/ETA, cancellation and net contribution by metro/county; avoid using customer identity/wealth or confidential rival feeds. Weekly (or slower) update geographic/daypart prices ONLY when enough direct observations have been gathered and independent holdouts/consumer transparency and safety limits pass. Start with weekday-rush, ordinary weekday, weekend and major-event categories, but **no fabricated fixed percent uplift**. Simulate 2 passes with severe demand, stale samples and tolls before proposing enabled price factors. One server-authoritative, consented Total, never a post-payment increase.
