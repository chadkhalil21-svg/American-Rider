# Market-data procurement rule

American Rider uses a public-first, licensed-gap-fill strategy.

1. Use authoritative government/open data at zero acquisition cost where it contains the fields required for the intended decision.
2. Use independent controlled audits/field panels to validate local conditions and fill modest gaps.
3. Purchase a licensed national/metro dataset only for coverage, freshness or passenger-charge fields that the public layer cannot supply.
4. Never buy data merely because it exists. A commercial source must materially improve a named commissioning gap and its permitted use must include American Rider's intended pricing analysis.

## Gridwise

Gridwise's worker app pricing is irrelevant to this procurement decision. Gridwise Analytics is the business product of interest. Its public site advertises national/metro customer-pricing analysis, weekly updates and 60+ markets, but does not publish business pricing. Treat cost as unknown until a written quote is received.

Before purchase require:
- market list and geographic granularity;
- record-level schema/data dictionary;
- customer-charge fields and treatment of tolls/taxes/fees/tips/promotions;
- service-class/product identification;
- timestamp/daypart resolution and latency;
- trip distance/duration and origin/destination granularity;
- historical depth and weekly delivery mechanism/API/export;
- permitted use for internal competitive pricing/calibration;
- retention/audit rights and provenance;
- price, minimum term, usage limits and termination/export rights;
- sample data sufficient to run American Rider's calibration and hold-out tests before contract.

## Decision gate

Do not contract if the public/independent evidence for the intended launch markets already satisfies the calibration gates. Contract only when the incremental markets/freshness/fields justify the cost.

No commercial provider becomes a single point of truth: preserve government and independent evidence as cross-checks.
